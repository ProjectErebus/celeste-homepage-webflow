First, the moment vessels forget

The next handover writes itself.

(02)

If you've ever inherited a vessel mid-rotation, you know what this moment is. The previous chief is on a plane. Their phone is off. You have an engine room and a verbal briefing that was meant to take a week and took two days.

The handover lives somewhere — usually in a Word document compiled at midnight before the rotation. Often nowhere at all. The handover that writes itself is what CelesteOS does first. Here are the five moments where it happens.

Moment 01 · During normal work

The daily prompt

An engineer submits a fault, completes a work order, logs a note. A small prompt appears. One tap to add it to the handover draft. One tap to dismiss. Never asked again if you say no.

Moment 02 · Eight months later

The draft accumulates

Every "yes" from the daily prompt adds an entry. By the time the chief rotates, the draft has eight months of operational context. Organised by department. Zero dedicated writing time.

Moment 03 · The last week

Review and edit

The outgoing chief opens the draft. The system has done the heavy lifting. This is a review task, not a writing task — read each entry, add context where needed, remove what's irrelevant, sign off.

Moment 04 · Sign-off

Both sides sign.

The outgoing chief signs. The incoming chief acknowledges with a critical-acknowledgement step — "I have read and understood." The captain is notified. Nobody can claim later that they weren't told.

Moment 05 · Permanent

Sealed. Independently verifiable.

The handover is now a permanent record. A surveyor, an insurer, a buyer can click the SealMark to verify it at verify.celeste7.ai without an account. The proof is independent of CelesteOS.

Eight months of operational context. Captured during normal work, not at midnight before the rotation. Sealed at sign-off. The vessel remembers what the crew is about to forget.
