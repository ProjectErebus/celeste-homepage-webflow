The gap between compliance and proof

Most planned maintenance systems satisfy compliance at the task level: a job was completed, a checklist was signed. What they rarely preserve is the chain of decisions between tasks — and that gap is where liability grows.

What an append-only record captures

The ledger is append-only. Nothing is ever overwritten. Corrections are recorded as new entries referencing the original. The original remains intact.

Every action — logging a fault, creating a work order, signing a handover — is attributed by signed-in user and role, and timestamped. Open any record and read its full audit trail.

Why immutability is not optional

Records that can be retroactively modified cannot serve as evidence. Deleted rows are struck through, never purged — the trail is the trail. The audit trail is not a feature. It is a legal defence.

Frequently asked questions

Can entries be edited after the fact?

No. The record is append-only; a correction is a new entry that references the original, which remains intact.

How is each action attributed?

By signed-in user and role, with a timestamp — who acted, what they did, when, and which record was affected.

Can the history be independently checked?

Every record's history is available on demand, and independently verifiable at verifier.celeste7.ai.
