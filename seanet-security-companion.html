About this document

This document describes how vessel records held in CelesteOS are protected, and how each protection can be verified. Each section opens with a summary. Technical detail for IT advisers follows.

Some information is deliberately withheld, including server addresses, configuration settings and anything else that could assist an attempt against a vessel. Those details are shared with customers and independent testers under a signed agreement.

01 · Comparison

How does CelesteOS compare with the usual approach?

Most vessels rely on shared passwords, emailed files and records without history. CelesteOS replaces each of these with a protection that can be verified.

In plain terms

  • One shared password on the ship's computer
  • Anyone with the password sees everything
  • Files emailed between departments and copied to USB drives
  • Records overwritten, with no history
  • Access remains after crew leave
  • Security set up once, then left alone
  • Assurances that cannot be checked
  • Each crew member signs in individually, through Microsoft 365, with a second device
  • Crew see only their own vessel, within the limits of their role
  • One copy of each file, fingerprinted and held securely; the original is left as it was
  • Every change recorded with its author and time. Nothing is erased
  • Access ends when the crew member is removed from the vessel
  • Security reviewed continuously. Every finding is corrected and re-tested
  • Protection that can be verified independently

How it is built

  • Individual identities through Entra ID SSO, with MFA enforced by the customer's Conditional Access.
  • Authorisation is role-based and vessel-scoped, resolved server-side on every request.
  • SHA-256 content fingerprinting at the point of capture.
  • An append-only audit ledger with a SHA-256 proof per event.
  • Deprovisioning: deactivating the CelesteOS account ends access; removing the Entra identity prevents re-authentication.
  • A continuous adversarial review programme, in which every finding is remediated and re-tested.
01 · THE DIFFERENCE, VERIFIABLE

02 · Layers

What protects a vessel's records?

Seven independent layers. Each is a different kind of protection, and access requires passing all of them.

In plain terms

  1. Microsoft 365 sign-in, with a second device. A stolen password is not sufficient without the second device.
  2. The vessel's approved crew list. A valid sign-in that is not on the list is refused.
  3. A signed access pass. After sign-in, every request carries a digitally signed pass. Any alteration invalidates it.
  4. Identity confirmed by our server. The vessel and role are never accepted from the user's device. Our server looks them up on every request.
  5. Every query limited to the vessel. Each request returns only records belonging to the user's vessel.
  6. Protection inside the database. Every table enforces its own access rules. Any attempt to reach the database without passing through our server is refused.
  7. Two separate databases. Sign-in information and vessel records are held apart. Access to one does not provide access to the other.

In addition, every action is recorded with its author and time, and fingerprinted.

How it is built

  • Defence in depth, with independent controls at the identity, session, application, database and infrastructure layers.
  • Identity: Entra ID SSO, customer-enforced MFA, and a per-vessel allow-list with explicit account linking.
  • Session: JWT signed with ES256 and verified against published public keys, with issuer and audience checked.
  • Authorisation: vessel and role resolved server-side on every request. Token claims are never trusted for access decisions.
  • Application: every query is scoped to the server-resolved vessel identifier. Cross-vessel references in write payloads are validated and rejected.
  • Database: row-level security on every table, with restrictive vessel-isolation and active-user policies.
  • Infrastructure: separate identity and operational databases. Service credentials are held server-side only.
  • Detection: an append-only audit ledger with a SHA-256 proof hash for every event.

Questions owners ask

If a crew member's phone is lost or stolen, can someone use it to get in?
No. The phone alone is not enough. The person would also need the crew member's Microsoft password, and the account must be on the vessel's approved crew list. Removing the crew member from the list ends access.
Is one layer more important than the others?
No. Each layer does a different job, and access requires passing all of them. That is the purpose of using several different kinds of protection.
02 · SEVEN INDEPENDENT LAYERS

03 · Sign-in

Who can sign in?

Only approved crew, signing in through the fleet's own Microsoft 365 with a second device.

In plain terms

  • Crew sign in on Microsoft's own sign-in page, using the work account the fleet already uses. CelesteOS never receives or stores the password.
  • Microsoft requires confirmation on a second device, typically a phone. This requirement is set in the fleet's own Microsoft 365 and remains under the control of its IT team. It is confirmed with them during onboarding.
  • Each user must also be on the vessel's approved crew list in CelesteOS.
  • Removing a crew member from the vessel's crew list ends their access. Removing them from Microsoft 365 prevents them signing in again.
  • Vessels without Microsoft 365 may sign in with an email address and password. Passwords are stored only in an irreversible, protected form, and cannot be read by anyone, including CelesteOS.

How it is built

  • SSO via Microsoft Entra ID (OpenID Connect). MFA is enforced by the customer's Conditional Access policy.
  • Microsoft identities must be explicitly linked to a provisioned, active CelesteOS account. A matching verified email alone is denied.
  • JWT signed with ES256, verified against published public keys, with issuer and audience checked.
  • Role and vessel are resolved server-side from records the user cannot modify.
  • Email and password fallback uses salted, hashed password storage.

Questions owners ask

We do not use Microsoft 365. Can we still use CelesteOS?
Yes. Crew can sign in with an email address and password. Passwords are stored only in an irreversible, protected form, and the same approved crew list applies.
What happens when a crew member rotates off or leaves?
Remove them from the vessel's crew list in CelesteOS, and their access ends. Removing them from Microsoft 365 as well prevents them signing in again.
Can a crew member give their login to a relief crew member?
No sign-in is shared. Each person signs in as themselves, on their own second device, and must be on the approved crew list. Every action is recorded against the person who took it.
03 · APPROVED CREW ONLY

04 · Website

What is visible on the public website?

A sign-in screen and nothing more.

In plain terms

  • The website presents the application's screens only. It contains no vessel records, passwords or credentials. All data is held behind our server and is released only after sign-in.
  • This can be confirmed in under a minute: open app.celeste7.ai, select "View page source", and search the code for any password or key.

How it is built

  • The frontend is a static bundle containing no secrets or customer data.
  • HTTPS is enforced with HSTS (two years, includeSubDomains, preload), a strict referrer policy, nosniff, and a content security policy with frame-ancestors 'none'.
  • Unauthenticated API requests are refused.

Questions owners ask

Could someone find our data by searching the internet?
No. Vessel records are never on the public website and are not indexed by search engines. They are released only to a signed-in, approved crew member.
04 · NOTHING TO FIND

05 · Location

Where is vessel data held?

In two separate databases in the United States. One confirms identity, the other holds vessel records.

In plain terms

  • The identity database confirms who a user is and which vessel they belong to. It holds no vessel records.
  • The records database holds work orders, faults, parts, certificates, documents and history.
  • The browser signs in against the identity database only. It cannot search or read the records database. Only our server can access it, and only once identity has been confirmed. When a document is opened, our server issues a link to that single file, and the link expires within one hour.
  • The credentials that operate CelesteOS are held only on our servers. They are never present in the website, on the vessel, or in any correspondence.

How it is built

  • One database for identity, user-to-vessel mapping and role. A separate database for operational records. Both are held in the United States.
  • Verified live: the deployed website has no query access to the records database, and the records database refuses sign-in tokens presented directly.
  • Encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2 or higher) and at rest (AES-256). Daily backups.
  • Service credentials exist only in server-side environments.
  • Sub-processors are named in the Data Processing Agreement.

Questions owners ask

Which country is our data held in?
The United States.
Is our data backed up?
Yes. The records database is backed up daily.
Who hosts the system?
Established cloud infrastructure providers. Each is named as a sub-processor in the Data Processing Agreement.
05 · TWO SEPARATE DATABASES

06 · Separation

How is each vessel's data kept separate?

Every record carries its vessel's identifier, and several independent checks ensure users see only their own vessel.

In plain terms

  • Every record is labelled with the vessel it belongs to.
  • On every request, our server identifies the user's vessel independently and returns only that vessel's records. A request for another vessel's record returns nothing.
  • Every table in the database also enforces its own access rules. Any attempt to bypass our server is refused.
  • A fleet of three vessels and a fleet of thirty receive the same separation.

How it is built

  • Multi-tenant, with a vessel identifier on every operational row.
  • Application layer: every query is scoped to the server-resolved vessel. Cross-vessel foreign keys in write payloads are rejected.
  • Database layer: Row-Level Security is enabled on every table, with restrictive vessel-isolation and active-user policies. Verified live: an anonymous caller receives 0 rows, a vessel A user receives 0 vessel B rows across six domains, and a cross-vessel insert is refused.
  • Adversarial reviews include a multi-agent red-team exercise (June 2026) and a full review of 470 database policies (September 2026). Every finding was corrected and re-tested.

Questions owners ask

Can the co-owners or crew of one SeaNet vessel see another SeaNet vessel's records?
No. Crew see only their own vessel. Only the fleet managers of a fleet can see the vessels in that fleet. During onboarding, we agree with you which vessels are grouped together, either as a single fleet or as several separate fleets, to match how SeaNet is organised.
Does the separation weaken as the fleet grows?
No. Every vessel receives the same separation, whether the fleet has three vessels or thirty.
06 · EACH VESSEL, ITS OWN RECORDS

07 · Identifiers

Can a record be reached by guessing its identifier?

No. Record identifiers are long random values, and a correct identifier is still refused unless it belongs to the user's vessel.

How it is built

  • Operational records use random 128-bit UUIDs.
  • Access is always authorised by vessel scope, never by possession of an identifier.
07 · SCOPE, NOT GUESSWORK

08 · AI

How is AI used, and how is its output controlled?

CelesteOS reads and organises vessel documents. Crew decide what enters the record, and every result shows its source.

Most yacht systems depend on crew entering information by hand. CelesteOS reads manuals, certificates, invoices and logs as they arrive, and connects each one to the equipment, parts, suppliers and history it relates to.

How the output is controlled

  • The system proposes. Crew decide. Once a vessel is live, nothing is added to, changed on or removed from its record without the action of a signed-in crew member whose role permits it. Records created during onboarding are read from the vessel's own files, and each links to its source.
  • Every result shows its source. Each search result links to the document or record it came from.
  • The original is retained. Each document is stored as received. The system's reading is held alongside it.
  • Every change is recorded, with its author and time.

How vessel data is protected

  • Celeste does not train AI on vessel data. AI is reached from Celeste's servers only, never from the browser.
  • The AI receives only the text required for the task at hand, never the vessel's full record.
  • Email addresses, telephone numbers and payment card numbers are masked before text is indexed or read.
  • AI services are reached only from our server, never from the user's browser.

How it is built

  • AI is used server-side for OCR, reading, embeddings and entity linking, through a single central client.
  • After onboarding, every write to the operational record is a user-initiated action, authenticated and checked against the user's role. Onboarding writes are pipeline-generated and carry their source document.
  • AI providers used in processing are named as sub-processors in the Data Processing Agreement.
  • Email and telephone masking before embedding. Payment card redaction before extraction.

Questions owners ask

Does the AI change our records on its own?
No. Once the vessel is live, nothing is added to, changed on or removed from its record without the action of a signed-in crew member. Records created during onboarding are read from the vessel's own files, and each links to its source.
What if the system reads a document incorrectly?
Every result links to the document it came from, so crew can check the source directly. Nothing enters the record without a crew member's action.
Is our data used to train ChatGPT or any other AI model?
No. Celeste does not use vessel data to train AI models. The AI providers used in processing are named in the Data Processing Agreement.
08 · THE SYSTEM PROPOSES. CREW DECIDE.

09 · Integrity

Can a record be altered after the fact?

Every change is recorded permanently. Sealed records carry a fingerprint and an independent timestamp, so any later alteration is detectable.

In plain terms

  • Every action is written to a log that is only ever added to, recording what happened, who did it and when. Corrections are made as new entries. Earlier entries remain.
  • Each log entry carries its own digital fingerprint.
  • Sealed records, such as a handover or a certificate pack, are stamped with a fingerprint of their entire contents and a timestamp from an independent time authority. Any subsequent alteration, by any party including CelesteOS, causes verification to fail.
  • Sealed records are made to be shared. A family office, a surveyor or an auditor can confirm at verifier.celeste7.ai that a record is genuine and unaltered, without a CelesteOS account and without being given access to the vessel's system.

How it is built

  • An append-only audit ledger with a SHA-256 proof hash for every event.
  • Sealed receipts use a Merkle root over the included events at seal time, a PDF digital signature and an RFC 3161 trusted timestamp.
  • The public verifier requires no CelesteOS account. It checks the document's hash, signature and timestamp, and grants no access to the underlying records.

Questions owners ask

Can CelesteOS alter a sealed record without us knowing?
No. Any change to a sealed record, by any party including CelesteOS, causes verification to fail.
Can we send a record to our family office or surveyor without giving them access to CelesteOS?
Yes. That is what sealed records are for. The recipient receives the record itself and can confirm it is genuine and unaltered at verifier.celeste7.ai. They see nothing else, and need no account.
Can a crew member delete an entry to hide a mistake?
No. Corrections are made as new entries, and the earlier entry remains in the record.
09 · CHANGES ARE DETECTABLE

10 · File transfer

How are files transferred from the vessel?

A copy is taken and fingerprinted. Documents on the vessel are never changed, moved or deleted.

In plain terms

  • A secure agent on the vessel monitors only the folders the vessel selects, and copies new or changed files.
  • It never edits or deletes a file on the vessel.
  • Each file is fingerprinted when copied. All files are re-checked weekly.
  • The agent holds no access to our databases. It holds a single vessel credential, stored in the computer's own secure credential store, which it can exchange only for a short-lived, upload-only permit.
  • Installation requires a code sent to the owner's email address.
  • The agent transmits only the files, their folder location and their fingerprints. Its routine status reports contain counts only, never file names.
  • It continues to operate through loss of connection and resumes when connection returns. If a drive appears empty or disconnected, it pauses rather than treating the files as deleted.

How it is built

  • SHA-256 fingerprint of every file, with a weekly full re-check.
  • The vessel credential is held in the operating system’s secure credential store, never in a plain file.
  • Uploads use short-lived, upload-only permits. Each request for a permit is signed and time-stamped, and a replayed request is rejected. Permits are held in memory only and can be revoked.
  • Activation requires a time-limited, rate-limited one-time code sent to the owner’s email.
  • Folder boundary enforcement, including symbolic links.
  • TLS for all traffic. Safeguards prevent files being treated as deleted when a drive is disconnected or appears empty.

Questions owners ask

Does the agent change or delete files on the vessel?
No. It copies files and never edits, moves or deletes them. Its only write is a small marker file in the chosen root folder, identifying the watched folder.
What happens when the vessel loses its internet connection?
The agent pauses and resumes when the connection returns. Nothing is lost, and files are not treated as deleted while the vessel is offline.
Does it send everything on the computer?
No. It copies only the folders the vessel chooses.
10 · COPIED, NEVER EDITED

11 · Crew personal data

How is crew personal data handled?

CelesteOS holds only the personal data needed to operate the vessel.

In plain terms

  • CelesteOS stores crew names, roles, work email addresses and, where provided, a work telephone number.
  • CelesteOS has no fields for passport numbers, bank details or medical records.
  • Documents uploaded by the vessel are protected in the same way as every other record, and remain under the vessel's control.
  • Crew retain their rights under UK and EU GDPR, including access, correction and erasure.

How it is built

  • Personal data schema: name, role, work email, and an optional telephone number. There are no passport, bank, medical or date-of-birth fields.
  • Lawful basis and controller and processor roles are set out in the DPA.

Questions owners ask

Can a crew member ask to see or delete their personal data?
Yes. Crew retain their rights under UK and EU GDPR. Requests are handled as set out in the Data Processing Agreement.
11 · ONLY WHAT IS NEEDED

12 · End of contract

What is returned at the end of a contract?

The data belongs to the vessel. Original files and records are returned, then deleted from CelesteOS within 30 days. Sealed records remain verifiable after the contract ends.

How it is built

  • Original source files are returned as uploaded.
  • Records and history are exported in standard formats.
  • Customer data is deleted within 30 days of its return.
  • The process is set out in the DPA.

Questions owners ask

Do we get our original files back exactly as we uploaded them?
Yes. Original files are returned as uploaded, together with the records and their history.
What happens to our data after the contract ends?
It is returned to you, then deleted from CelesteOS within 30 days.
12 · RETURNED, THEN DELETED

13 · Incidents

How are incidents detected and reported?

Any incident affecting vessel data is reported to the customer within 72 hours of confirmation.

In plain terms

  • A documented incident plan defines responsibilities, severity levels and notification.
  • The system can be placed into a protective mode immediately while an incident is investigated.
  • Security is reviewed continuously. Each finding is corrected, re-tested and recorded, and protections are updated as threats change.

How it is built

  • An incident response runbook covering roles, severity levels, and the 72-hour GDPR notification requirement.
  • An incident-mode control that restricts the system during investigation.
  • Security controls follow the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria and ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A. Not yet formally certified.

Questions owners ask

How quickly would we be told about a problem?
Within 72 hours of an incident affecting vessel data being confirmed.
Who do we contact with a security concern?
contact@celeste7.ai
Is CelesteOS certified to SOC 2 or ISO 27001?
CelesteOS is built and operated to the SOC 2 and ISO 27001 frameworks, and follows their controls. It is working towards formal certification.
Does CelesteOS comply with GDPR?
Yes. CelesteOS processes personal data in line with UK and EU GDPR, as set out in the Data Processing Agreement.
