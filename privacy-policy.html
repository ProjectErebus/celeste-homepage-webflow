8. Artificial Intelligence Features

8.1 What the AI features do. The Service includes features powered by third-party AI model providers, which provide:

  • search and answers with source citations;
  • document classification and data extraction;
  • reading text from scanned documents;
  • drafting handover notes from user notes;
  • suggesting related documents; and
  • finding warranty information.

8.2 What is sent. When these features run, the text of the relevant records or documents, or page images, is sent to the AI model provider. We send only what each feature reasonably needs. We do not remove names or vessel names before sending. The only exception is that we mask email addresses and telephone numbers in text used to build search indexes.

8.3 No training. Our contracts with, or the published terms of, our AI model providers do not permit them to use content sent through the Service to train their models.

8.4 Retention by providers. Some providers keep inputs for a limited period for abuse monitoring, which for at least one provider is up to 30 days, before deleting them.

8.5 Outputs can be wrong. AI outputs can be inaccurate or incomplete. The Service does not use AI to make decisions about individuals that have legal or similarly significant effects. Decisions remain with the vessel's Master, crew and the Customer.

9. The Collective

9.1 What it is. The Collective is an engineering-knowledge service about equipment failures and fixes. The Collective Site displays Public-Source Content (Section 10). Once fleet-contributed insights are published, participating Customers will also see de-identified insights built from the contributions of participating fleets there. Participating Customers contribute engineering information from their own records so that the whole fleet can learn which problems recur and what solved them. Sections 9.2 to 9.4 describe how that works. A Customer's data is never used for the Collective unless that Customer has opted in.

9.2 Opt-in by Customers only. A Customer's data is used for the Collective only if that Customer opts in through its contract with us. Individual crew members do not opt in or out themselves. Questions about whether your organization participates should go to your organization.

9.3 What is shared. If a Customer opts in, before anything is shared:

  • the Customer's identity is replaced with a pseudonym;
  • identifiers are removed by rule, including names, email addresses, vessel names, vessel identification numbers, call signs and equipment serial numbers;
  • free-text notes are excluded by default; and
  • nothing is published unless at least five independent fleets have reported the same failure and fix.

What participants will see are de-identified engineering patterns, not crew information and not any one vessel's records.

9.4 Pseudonymized is not anonymous. The pseudonymized contributions are not anonymous, and we protect them as personal information where they relate to an identifiable person. Insights published to participants are de-identified.

9.5 Signing in to the Collective Site. You sign in to the Collective Site with your CelesteOS account. We use the same account system as the Service and process your sign-in information as described in Section 2.4. While you are signed in, your browser keeps a session token in local storage so that you stay signed in; it is removed when you sign out. We do not use analytics or advertising tools on the Collective Site, and we do not record your searches for any purpose other than delivering results and keeping the site secure.

10. Information We Collect from Public Sources (Collective)

10.1 What we collect and from where. For the Collective, we collect posts, reports and other content that has been published on publicly accessible websites. Our sources include public accident-investigation and incident reports, industry safety notices, maritime and yachting news sites, public boating and engineering forums and discussion boards, public question-and-answer sites, public video and code-hosting platforms, and published equipment manuals. From these sources we may collect:

  • the user name, display name or author name under which the content was published;
  • the title, text and date of the post or report, and a link to it where available; and
  • any personal information that the author or others included in the content, such as a person's name or a description of an incident. Such content may occasionally include information about health or injuries, for example in accident reports.

10.2 Why we collect it. We use this information to build a searchable knowledge base of reported equipment faults, the remedies people tried, and the reported outcomes, so that vessel engineers can find relevant experience quickly (Section 3(k)). We do not use it to contact, profile or market to the people who wrote it, and we do not combine it with other information to identify them.

10.3 How we process it. Our automated tools collect the content and then use AI models to extract technical fields (such as equipment, manufacturer, model and root cause) and to label reported outcomes. At present, these AI models run on our own equipment, and we do not send Public-Source Content to third-party AI model providers. If that changes, we will update this Policy first.

10.4 Who can see it. Public-Source Content is shown to signed-in users of the Collective Site, with a link to the original source where we have one. It may include the user name or display name under which the content was originally published.

10.5 How we limit personal information. We remove email addresses and telephone numbers from the text of collected content. We use automated tools to detect and remove other people's names from the text where they can, but these tools do not catch every name. User names and titles are currently displayed as they appeared in the original source. We are updating our collection process so that author names and handles are no longer stored, and are working to remove them from existing records. We will remove or reduce any item on request (Section 10.8).

10.6 Legal basis and this notice (EEA, UK and Switzerland). We rely on our legitimate interests, and the interests of the vessel engineers who use the Collective, in making publicly reported technical experience about equipment faults easier to find, which supports safe and reliable vessel maintenance. We have balanced these interests against the interests of the people concerned, taking into account that the content was published publicly in a technical context, the measures in Section 10.5, and your right to object. You can ask us for details of this assessment. This Section 10 is our notice to the people whose information we collect from public sources, as required by Article 14 of the GDPR and UK GDPR. We generally do not hold contact details for the authors of public posts, so contacting each of them individually would be impossible or would involve disproportionate effort, and would require us to collect more personal information about them. We therefore make this information publicly available here instead.

10.7 Retention and location. We keep Public-Source Content for as long as the item remains useful to the knowledge base, and we remove it promptly on a valid objection or takedown request (Section 10.8). It is stored with our primary hosting provider in Germany. Collection and processing also take place on our own equipment in the United States.

10.8 Your choices and rights. If you believe your information appears in Public-Source Content, you can ask us to remove it, correct it, give you a copy, or stop processing it, by emailing contact@celeste7.ai with the user name or a link to the item or the original post. In the EEA, UK and Switzerland, you have the right to object to our processing based on legitimate interests (Section 18.4). We will stop displaying an item promptly while we review a request, and we will remove your information unless we have compelling legitimate grounds to keep it or the law requires otherwise. If you have deleted or edited the original post, please tell us, and we will update or remove our copy. These rights apply in addition to the rights in Sections 17 and 18.

10.9 US state privacy laws. Some US state privacy laws, including California's, exclude "publicly available information" from the personal information they regulate. Under California law, that exclusion covers only information that is lawfully made available from government records, or that a business has a reasonable basis to believe is lawfully made available to the general public by the person concerned or by widely distributed media. Not all Public-Source Content may meet that test. Whether or not an exclusion applies, we will honor requests about Public-Source Content as described in Section 10.8. We do not sell or share Public-Source Content.

10.10 Copyright and other requests. Requests from authors or rights holders about the content itself, rather than personal information, can be made under our Terms of Use.

11. The Verifier

11.1 Your file is never uploaded. The Verifier lets anyone check whether the content of a sealed record export matches the integrity digests recorded in it. It runs entirely in your web browser. The file you check is never uploaded to us, and the Verifier makes no network requests during the check and uses no cookies, browser storage or analytics.

11.2 Page hosting. When your browser loads the Verifier page, our hosting provider receives standard technical information, such as your IP address and browser type, as for any web page. That information is used only to deliver and secure the page. The Verifier's fonts are served from our own site, and the Verifier loads no files from third parties.

11.3 What the check covers. The Verifier checks the file's internal content integrity. It does not currently validate the digital signature.

12. The App on Your Device: Browser Storage and Offline Access

12.1 What is stored on your device. To keep you signed in and to let you work on board with poor connectivity, the Service stores the following in your browser:

(a) your session token, user identifier, email address, role and vessel name, in your browser's local storage; and

(b) copies of records you have viewed, for up to 7 days, in an offline cache. The cache is kept separately for each user and each vessel, is deleted when you sign out, and never includes crew certificates.

12.2 Not encrypted by the app. This stored information is not encrypted by the Service on your device. Anyone with access to your unlocked device or browser profile could read it. Please use device encryption and a screen lock, and sign out on shared devices. Your organization may have its own device rules.

12.3 Push notifications. If you enable push notifications, your browser registers with its push service (operated by your browser or device maker), and notification titles and text pass through that service.

14. Security

We use administrative, technical and organizational measures designed to protect personal information, including:

  • encryption in transit;
  • encrypted backups;
  • access controls that separate each Customer's and vessel's data; and
  • monitoring for misuse.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee the security of personal information.

Security breaches. If a breach of security affects personal information that we control, we will notify affected individuals and regulators as the law requires. For example, for residents of Florida, we will notify affected individuals no later than 30 days after we determine that a breach occurred or have reason to believe one occurred (unless law enforcement asks us to delay or the law allows a short extension), and we will notify the Florida Department of Legal Affairs where 500 or more Florida residents are affected. If a breach affects Customer Data, we notify the affected Customer without undue delay, with a target of 48 hours after we confirm the breach, so that the Customer can meet its own notification duties.

Please tell us promptly at contact@celeste7.ai (subject line "Security") if you believe your account has been compromised.
